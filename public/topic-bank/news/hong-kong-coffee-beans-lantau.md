# 默默深耕的宏大夢想：在寸土寸金的香港開闢本土咖啡種植路 | A quietly ambitious mission to grow coffee in Hong Kong

烘豆機在大嶼山手工咖啡品牌 LCC Roastery 的閣樓裡像一列怠速運轉的火車般發出嗡嗡聲，吐出新鮮烘焙的咖啡豆。

A coffee roaster hums like an idling train in the attic of LCC Roastery, churning out freshly cooked beans at the artisan coffee seller on Hong Kong’s Lantau Island.

店主林駒（Ringo Lam，譯音）對店內琳瑯滿目的咖啡豆選擇感到無比自豪：標著「衣索比亞」的玻璃罐承諾帶來「茉莉花、花香、熱帶水果、鳳梨」的混合風味，另一個來自哥倫比亞的玻璃罐則散發著「奶油、焦糖、黑巧克力」的香氣。但其中一款最為吸睛：標著「大嶼山豆」（Lantau Bean）的玻璃罐。

Owner Ringo Lam is proud of the broad selection of beans on offer: a jar labeled “Ethiopia” promises a mix of “jasmine, floral, tropical fruit, pineapple” flavors. Another, from Colombia, offers hints of “butter, caramel, dark chocolate.” But one stands out: “Lantau Bean.”

那個罐子裝載著林駒與其他咖啡愛好者大膽的開拓夢想：在繁華大都市的陰影下，種植出許多人認為不可能實現的咖啡豆。

That jar holds Lam and his fellow coffee lovers’ daring dream to pioneer what many didn’t know was possible: growing coffee in the shadow of a metropolis.

這位現年 55 歲的前科技創業家一直與大嶼山（一個以綠意盎然和悠閒生活著稱的島嶼，距離香港繁華市中心僅 30 分鐘渡輪航程）的農民密切合作，致力於培育出屬於這座城市自己的咖啡豆。

The 55-year-old former tech entrepreneur has been working closely with farmers on Lantau –– an island known for its greenery and laidback lifestyle, a 30-minute ferry ride from Hong Kong’s bustling city center –– in a quest to cultivate the city’s very own beans.

亞洲出產一些世界上最優質的咖啡，主要分佈在東南亞，那裡的熱帶氣候為越南和印尼等國家提供了理想的種植環境。

Asia produces some of the world’s finest coffee, mostly in the southeastern part of the continent where a tropical climate blesses countries like Vietnam and Indonesia with a conducive environment.

在東亞，大眾對這款飲品的喜愛在過去十年呈指數級增長。但較為不利的氣候條件——即每年固定的極端寒冷期——阻礙了日本和中國大陸等地區大面積發展咖啡作物（除了雲南高海拔山區或台灣阿里山山脈等少數地區，那裡依然能培育出頂級阿拉比卡豆）。

In East Asia, love for the drink has grown exponentially in the past decade. But less favorable conditions –– namely, annual periods of extreme cold –– have hindered countries such as Japan and China from developing their crops (save for a few high-altitude, mountainous areas like the Yunnan region in mainland China or Alishan Mountain Range in Taiwan, where premium Arabica can still thrive.)

香港這座擁有 750 萬人口的城市，大部分居民生活在城市區，擁有 700 多家咖啡館，但從未被視為種植咖啡的理想之地。除了夏季颱風的干擾外，高昂的土地價格也使得這座金融樞紐進口絕大部分糧食與蔬菜，而非自行種植，更遑論咖啡豆了。

Hong Kong, a Chinese city of 7.5 million people who live mostly in urban areas, has more than 700 cafes but has never been seen as an ideal breeding ground for coffee beans. It has a stronger cultural attachment to tea and – disruptive summer typhoons aside – exorbitant land prices have made it more logical for the financial hub to import almost all of its own grains and vegetables rather than grow them. Let alone coffee beans.

因此，當林駒向人們透露他的宏大計劃時，往往會引來一片困惑。「在大家的認知裡，咖啡產地都是那些你可能不打算去旅遊的國家，」林駒向 CNN 幽默地舉例說，「衣索比亞、哥倫比亞——這些絕對不是熱門的旅遊勝地。」「但突然之間，當有人在離你這麼近的地方種植咖啡時，大家就會驚奇地問：『我們真的能在香港種出咖啡嗎？』」

So when Lam tells people about his ambitious plan, he often leaves them in bewilderment. “All they see are just countries that you probably won’t plan to go,” Lam told CNN, jokingly giving a few examples. “Ethiopia, Colombia – that’s definitely not your top tourist places.” “But suddenly, when someone is growing coffee so close to you, they will ask, ‘Can we really grow coffee in Hong Kong?’” he said.

答案是：確實可以。雖然高海拔山區能增添風味層次與複雜性，但咖啡樹只能在高地茁壯成長其實是個誤解。香港大學公民社會與治理研究中心助理總監、同時也參與運行一家咖啡農場的戚曉麗（Katie Chick）表示，決定咖啡生長的是該地區是否屬於赤道南北各 25 度的「咖啡帶」，而香港位於北緯 22 度，正處於這一區間內。

The answer, it turns out, is yes. While high altitudes may boost flavor and complexity, it’s a misconception that coffee trees only thrive there. What determines their growth is whether the region falls under the so called the “coffee belt,” which is about 25 degrees north and south of the equator, says Katie Chick, an arboriculture instructor involved in running a coffee farm linked to the University of Hong Kong. Sitting 22 degrees north of the equator, the city is just within that band.

「從地理上來說，香港非常適合種植，我們只是缺乏海拔高度，」戚曉麗說。雖然世界上許多最著名的咖啡產區都在海拔 1000 公尺以上，但香港的最高峰也低於這個高度，且當地的農場多處於低平地帶。

“Geographically speaking, Hong Kong is fit to grow. We just lack a bit of altitude,” said Chick, the assistant director of the university’s Centre for Civil Society and Governance. While many of the world’s most popular coffee regions are at more than 1,000 meters above sea level, Hong Kong’s highest point is less than that, and its farms are low-lying.

戚曉麗解釋，高海拔山區劇烈的日夜溫差能激發咖啡豆產生更多生化反應，從而帶來更豐富的風味層次。「但這並非唯一要素，」她說。

Chick said daily temperatures fluctuate more drastically high up in mountainous areas, which may spark more biochemical reactions in the beans, leading to more a complex taste. “But that’s not the only requirement,” she said.

她的中心運營著香港最大的咖啡農場，擁有 800 棵咖啡樹，每年可產出高達 50 公斤的咖啡豆。這項計畫最初是為了振興一個古老村落的鄉村活力，如今戚曉麗和她的同事們已經開始在本地市場上銷售她們的咖啡豆。

Her center runs the biggest coffee farm in Hong Kong, with 800 trees yielding up to 50 kilograms of beans per year. It was originally envisioned as a project to revitalize an old village in the countryside, but now Chick and her colleagues are selling their beans at local markets.

對於林駒而言，這一切始於六年前的一次巴拿馬之旅。當時他前往當地拜訪農民並研究咖啡產業，在旅程結束時，他帶回了 100 顆咖啡種子。

For Lam, it all started on a trip to Panama six years ago. He was there to visit some farmers and study how the industry works. During that trip, he was given 100 coffee seeds to take home.

這場跨越太平洋的移植任務絕非易事：並非每一顆種子都會發芽，且咖啡樹往往需要兩到三年才能開花結果。「在所有 100 顆種子中，大約有 80 幾顆成功萌芽，」他說。他隨後聯繫了島上認識的每一位農夫，請求他們收留這些幼苗。

The cross-Pacific transplant mission was not a sure bet: not every seed will bud, and coffee plants often take two to three years to bear fruit. “Out of all those 100 seeds, about 80 something came out,” he said, telling of how he called up every farmer he knew on the island, asking them to take in the seedlings.

起初只有五位農民答應，後來陸續有更多人被說服加入。經過不斷的嘗試與摸索，他們成功呵護並養大了這些樹苗。如今，25 位農民在大嶼山照料著約 400 棵咖啡樹。今年初，他們迎來了有史以來最大規模的採收，共收穫 10 公斤咖啡豆，幾乎是 2023 年首次收成的 10 倍。

Initially, five farmers said yes, Lam recalled, with more convinced to join later. Through trial and error, they managed to preserve and grow the seedlings. Now, 25 farmers nurture about 400 coffee trees on Lantau Island. Earlier this year, they harvested their largest batch of coffee cherries yet, measuring 10 kilograms, almost 10 times their first yields in 2023.

一項年度聚會如今將大嶼山以及香港大學戚曉麗團隊等本地咖啡農人匯聚一堂，共同激盪改良種植技術的新點子。

An annual gathering now brings together local coffee farmers, including those on Lantau and Chick’s team from HKU, to brainstorm ways to refine their techniques.

儘管取得了成功，這些農民並非試圖顛覆由拉美和東南亞主導的全球咖啡貿易。事實上，由於生產成本極高，幾乎沒有人純粹將其作為商品出售，這在商業上很難盈利。此外，其產量與頂級生產國相比微不足道。林駒和他的農友今年創紀錄的 10 公斤收成，甚至裝不滿國際貿易中作為基本計量單位的 60 公斤咖啡袋。

Despite the success, these farmers aren’t seeking to upend a global coffee trade dominated by Latin America and Southeast Asia, or overthrow established growers. In fact, very few are selling their yields solely as a product, as the high costs of production make it hardly commercially viable. The yields are also nowhere near any of the top producing countries. The 10-kilograms record harvest Lam and his fellow farmers bagged this year wouldn’t fill a 60 kilograms bag used as the industry’s basic unit of trade.

相比之下，根據美國農業部的數據，全球最大咖啡種植國巴西的農民去年生產了 6,300 萬袋。

By comparison, farmers in Brazil, the world’s biggest coffee grower, produced 63 million of those bags last year, according to the US Department of Agriculture.

風味究竟如何？CNN 親自品嚐了兩款香港種植的咖啡，發現其口感滑順且易於入口，但與市中心專業咖啡館裡那些高複雜度的精品咖啡相比，風味層次確實略顯單薄。

And how does it taste? CNN samples of two separate Hong Kong-grown coffees revealed a smooth and easy drink, though lacking in the complexity of a batch you’d pick up at a city-center speciality brewer.

這促使這個剛剛起步的本地產業不斷創新——嘗試不同的水洗處理法以提升風味，並舉辦體驗坊以提高大眾認知，期盼能最大化本地作物的價值與影響力。

That forces many in the fledgling local industry to innovate –– trialing different wash processes to refine the taste and holding workshops to build awareness –– in the hope of maximizing the value and impact of their home-grown crops.

本地咖啡推廣團體「種到杯」（Seed to Cup）創辦人沈君亮（Mike Sim，譯音）正致力於在產量有限的情況下給人留下深刻印象。這位電腦工程師在香港北部的粉嶺租了一片農地，在努力改良咖啡豆的同時，也開展教育體驗工作坊。

Mike Sim, founder of Seed to Cup, a group that promotes local coffee, is finding ways to leave a lasting impression despite the Hong Kong origin not being widely available. The computer engineer has rented a farm in Fanling, in northern Hong Kong, where he works to perfect his beans while running education workshops on the side.

去年，他與一位咖啡師合作，在咖啡沖煮大賽中展示了一批本地種植並拼配了哥倫比亞品種的咖啡豆。雖然最終未能奪冠，但他認為這是一個巨大的飛躍。「我們向大眾展示了，香港現在有本地農場正在與咖啡師合作參與這些賽事，」他說，並表示今年他們計劃使用百分之百本地種植的咖啡豆參賽。

Last year, he partnered with a barista in a coffee-making contest to showcase a batch he grew, which was mixed with Colombian varietal. They didn’t win, but Sim touted it as a big step forward. “We showed people that there are farms in Hong Kong now working with baristas [in these competitions],” he said, adding that this year they intend to compete with solely locally grown beans.

大嶼山農夫之一陳鳳明（Chan Fung-ming，譯音）在幾年前辭去了社工工作，接管了家族的農田。她專攻園藝治療，倡導利用園藝改善人們的身心健康。她希望以咖啡為媒介，向年輕人介紹農耕文化。「我認為這是一個引領人們走進種植世界的極佳載體，」她說。

One of Lam’s Lantau farmers is Chan Fung-ming, who quit her job as a social worker to take over her family’s farm a few years ago. Specializing in horticultural therapy, she advocates the use of gardening to improve people’s well-being. She’s hoping to use coffee to introduce farming to youngsters. “I think it’s a medium to bring people into the world of planting,” she said.

除了烘焙坊外，林駒還開設了體驗工作坊，讓參與者親手採摘咖啡櫻桃並從頭開始加工。他表示，這能讓遊客切身體會到，那些遙遠產地的農夫為了給全球提供提神飲品，每天所經歷的艱辛勞作。

Alongside his roastery, Lam also runs a workshop that lets participants pick coffee cherries from trees and process them from scratch, which he says offers visitors a taste of the back-breaking routine farm workers in far-off places must go through every day to keep the world caffeinated.

林駒表示，此舉的目的是為了表明這些農夫值得更好的報酬——以及對其招牌產品的認可，就像紅酒歸功於其酒莊一樣。在這些工作坊中，他還會穿插講述自己的故事：他如何從一位把探訪咖啡館當作興趣的科技新創公司老闆，轉變為推動咖啡產業可持續發展的專職倡導者。

The aim, he says, is to show that these workers deserve better pay –– as well as acknowledgement for their signature offerings, much like wines are attributed to their winery. During these workshops, he also weaves in snippets of his own story: how he went from a tech startup boss reviewing cafes as a hobby, to a full-time advocate for the industry and its sustainability.

他說，每公斤咖啡豆（可沖泡約 44 杯咖啡）農夫只能拿到大約 2 到 3 美元，而許多生活在香港等大都市的人對此一無所知。「我們不會有足夠的土地來進行規模化種植，但至少在經歷了這個工作坊之後，大家會與咖啡的源頭產生更深的連結。」

For every kilogram of beans –– which can produce 44 cups of coffee –– farmers get about $2-3, he says, adding that this is what many living in major cities like Hong Kong are rarely aware of. “We won’t have enough land to [grow coffee at scale], but at least after going through this workshop and exercise, they will be more connected to the origin,” he said.

而林駒也見證了參與者的改變：「通常，他們都會把杯子裡的咖啡喝個精光。」在那之後，「他們或許也更願意多支付一點點費用了。」

And Lam has seen some changes in people who attended his sessions. “Usually, they will finish the cup,” he said. Afterwards, “they are probably more willing to pay a little bit more.”
